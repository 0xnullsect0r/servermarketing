# Competitors: 43 public ATM10 servers

Source: the All the Mods 10 modpack page on `moddedminecraftservers.com`
(`/m/all-the-mods-10.13177475/`, pages 1-3), read 2026-09-20. This is the only
ATM10-specific server directory I found that is both comprehensive and
machine-readable. It is a German-run site (ARK Forum) with free listings, a
points/votes system, and an optional paid "highlighted" tier.

This is, as far as I can tell, close to the complete population of publicly
listed ATM10 servers. That in itself is the most useful finding.

## What the shape of this data actually says

**1. The middle of this market is empty, and that is the opportunity.**

Two servers (BacoNetworks at Ø118 and the "ATM10 Community Server" at Ø244)
hold the overwhelming majority of all publicly listed ATM10 concurrency. After
those two, the *third* biggest is Ø17. Eleven of the 43 were flat offline when
I checked. Roughly two-thirds sit at an average of two players or fewer.

So the real competitive picture is not "43 competitors". It is:

- 2 large public servers, which are effectively a different product: big,
  anonymous, you are a stranger among hundreds.
- ~10 servers with a faint pulse (Ø 4-17).
- ~30 that are functionally dead or dying.

A server that reliably has 6-12 real people on it and does not disappear would
sit third or fourth in this entire category. That is a genuinely reachable
position, and it is a much more honest goal than "compete with the big two".

**2. Death is the normal outcome, and everybody browsing these lists knows it.**

Eleven offline, thirty at Ø<=2. Anyone who has looked for an ATM server has
been burned by joining one that emptied out within a fortnight. This is the
single strongest emotion in the audience and almost nobody is addressing it.

The competitor copy addresses "is it fun". It does not address "will you
still be here in March". Evidence of *durability* is the underserved claim:
scheduled restarts, real backups, a named owner, a stated position on world
resets, an uptime record. All of which this server actually has.

**3. The claims everyone makes, which are therefore worth nothing.**

From the names alone: "Chill", "Community Based", "Fun", "Be Nice - Have Fun",
"Friendly", "No P2W", "Small Community". "No P2W" appears in two server names
and is near-universal in ATM server descriptions, because ATM servers mostly
do not monetise at all. Saying "no pay-to-win" here is like a restaurant
advertising that it has chairs. It is true, it is expected, and it signals
nothing.

**Do not lead with any of these.** They are noise. Put them in a
specifications block at the bottom where a reader can tick them off, and
spend the headline on something falsifiable.

**4. Regional segmentation is common. Cross-regional coverage is claimed by nobody.**

Seven of the 43 put a language or region in the name or listing: PT x2, DE x3,
ES x2, CS x1, plus `[EU] Simple SMP` and `FULL ATM 10 LATAM`. The pattern is
always *exclusion*: this server is for people in this place who speak this
language.

Not one of the 43 claims coverage *across* timezones. Given that this server
has a US half and a New Zealand half, "there are people on when your local
server is empty" is a positioning statement that is currently unoccupied in
this entire category. See `product-audit.md` Finding 5 for the caveat: it
needs to be measured before it is claimed.

**5. Resets and wipes are a live, contested issue, and taking a side is free differentiation.**

Server 6 puts "(No resets)" in its name. Server 10 advertises "Wiped
01.01.2026". The directory has a "Last Reset" filter with buckets from 7 days
to 12 months, which means enough servers wipe often enough that a filter for
it earns its place.

For a player deciding where to spend fifty hours, "will my base still exist"
is a top-three question. A clear, public, permanent answer is worth more than
any adjective. If this server does not intend to wipe, that should be stated
explicitly and prominently. See `QUESTIONS.md` Q4.

**6. The two genuinely distinctive positions in the set, for reference.**

- **"ATM10 for Grownies"** (0/20, but the idea is sound): an explicit adult
  audience filter. It is a real segmentation, it self-selects for people with
  jobs who play at consistent hours, and it is honest.
- **"The Penguin Network [Time based ranks] [No P2W]"**: ranks earned by
  playtime rather than money. A concrete mechanism instead of an adjective.

Both work for the same reason: they describe a *rule*, not a *vibe*. Rules can
be verified by a skeptical reader. Vibes cannot.

## What nobody in this set is doing

Assembled from reading all 43 listings:

| Unclaimed position | Can this server honestly claim it? |
|---|---|
| Verified coverage across US and Pacific hours | Probably, pending one week of measurement |
| "Here is our uptime and restart schedule, in public" | Yes, this is already true |
| "Here is our backup policy, specifically" | Yes, backups exist; needs specifics |
| "The owner is a working software engineer and this is run like infrastructure" | Yes, and it is unusual and checkable |
| A permanent, public no-wipe commitment | Needs an owner decision |
| A real custom spawn shown rather than described | Yes, the build exists and is unphotographed |

The last one deserves emphasis. Of the 43 listings, essentially all describe
themselves in adjectives. A server that leads with *a photograph of a thing
somebody actually built* is doing something structurally different from its
entire competitive set, and it costs one screenshot.
